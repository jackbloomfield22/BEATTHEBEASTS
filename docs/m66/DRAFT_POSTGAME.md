# M6.6: draft, locker room, post-game and box score

The Playtest 1 and Playtest 2 items for the draft, the locker room, the results, the box score, the main menu and the pre-game moment (`docs/PLAYTEST-1.md`, `docs/PLAYTEST-2.md`). Branch `m66-draft-postgame`, off `claude/m65-gameplay`. No `src/sim` changes; the in-play HUD and the kits are untouched.

Screenshots are in `docs/screenshots/m6.6/`, captured with `tools/shots/m66.spec.ts` (software GL at 1920×1080 unless noted; the box score also at 1280×720). The results screens draw a real six-round game played through the sim in Node (`tools/shots/record-fixture.ts`, seed 13: the AI at the controls, the Beasts' possessions, the backfield rotation, the box score and the record exactly as the game screen builds them).

## What changed, item by item

### Draft and locker room

**Typography.** Inter was already the UI face, but the owner read the game as one font, and he was right in effect: Bungee carried every name, number, score, grade and sticker, and Inter only appeared in small caps labels that looked like the same voice. Now there are two typefaces with three jobs:
- Bungee (display) for headlines only: screen titles, the stage's verbs (Spin, Walk out, New draft), a player's name on the Scouting card, the locker's nameplates and jerseys.
- Barlow (body, OFL) for running text: the rules card, the scouting lines, the reasons, the menu.
- Barlow Semi Condensed (numbers, OFL) for every number and table: the box score, the scores, the grades, the slot strip, the reels, the stickers.
Both come from `@fontsource`, are credited in `CREDITS.md`, and `--font-ui` / `--font-num` in `global.css` carry them everywhere. The in-play HUD inherits the new body face through `--font-ui` (no HUD code changed).

**Spin readout.** Two sources of glare. The video wall multiplies its canvas by 2.2 to be the brightest thing in the room, so white type hit 2.2 in linear light, far past the room's bloom threshold (0.85 pregame, 0.75 lights down): the team and year haloed. Every text color on the wall is now scaled in linear light so its brightest channel lands at 0.62 after the multiply (`wallInk`, `TEXT_PEAK` in `videoWall.ts`; tested for white, lime, red and translucent white). The lit field behind the type keeps its level, so the wall is still the room's brightest surface. On the stage, the reel readout lost its lime text glow and now sets the team and decade in Barlow Semi Condensed 800, near-white on black with a lime rule when locked.

**Beasts panel.** The wall's all-eleven page drew one column of eleven rows, three labels and gaps: 790 px on a 720 px canvas, so the secondary ran off the bottom of the frame. It's two columns now (the front and the backers left, the secondary right), each man's name over his team and decade, names fitted to the column. The single-group pages are unchanged.

**Trait stickers.** `occupantOf` took `.slice(0, 2)` of the positive traits, and the canvas only had room for two rows at one size. Every trait goes on now (a negative one in warning red), plus the All-Pro count; the layout tries sizes from large to small until every sticker fits the cabinet face (`layoutStickers`, tested with six stickers). The O-line's stall wears its unit word and the five men's Pro Bowls as well.

**Rules card.** A card on the left at the start of every draft (the intro, while the Beasts are on the wall), gone on the first spin: how the spin works, the roster, Team Skip and Era Skip (or "no skips today" in the Daily), why a spot reads "2nd WR", and that the best man starts. The How to Play page says the same.

**Slot labels.** "WR2" read like a ranking. In the pick list a man now goes "→ 1st WR" or "→ 2nd TE"; a full position reads "WRs full" (the line: "Line set"); the Draft confirm says "Draft as your 2nd RB" or "Your RBs are full"; the top strip shows the position with "2 of 3" beside it; the dressing lower third shows just the position.

**O-line.** Numbers stay hidden. The Scouting card for a unit shows its five linemen by name with stickers for their All-Pro and Pro Bowl counts (career, from the cited accolades; Pro Bowls are new in `data/augment/honors.json`, generated by `tools/augment/honors.ts`: 1,599 entries), and one word for the unit. The word's rule (`unitWord` in `src/game/draftView.ts`): the unit's OVR, ranked against every rated OL unit in the pool (184); the top 10% are Elite, the next 25% Strong, the next 40% Solid, the bottom 25% Weak. The result carries its trace (the OVR, the pool size, the units below it, the cut). Film Room keeps the names and the honors (public record) but not the word, which is read off the ratings.

**Filled positions.** Struck through (a 2 px line through the name), faded, and the position chip greyed.

**Starters.** `src/game/depth.ts`: within each position group the slots are re-assigned by OVR, so RB1, WR1 and TE1 are the better players whatever the draft order; ties keep draft order. The locker keeps the pick order (the stall is where he signed); `draftedTeam` (so the sim and every snap), the record's lineup, the key matchups and the grades use the depth chart. Once the roster is full the top strip becomes the depth chart (RB1, RB2, WR1–3, TE1, TE2, with RB2 marked "3rd down" and TE2 "2-TE sets"). It only sorts after the roster is full, so it never hints at ratings mid-draft.

**RB2 and TE2 snaps.** Game layer only (`src/game/rotation.ts`, through a new `squad` hook the play engine asks each snap; the sim's personnel groupings then fill the eleven as always). RB2 is the back on screens, on passing downs (3rd or 4th and 5+), and on every third snap of a drive; the starter keeps the fullback sets (RB2 is already on the field there as the fullback) and short yardage. That puts RB2 on about a third of the early-down snaps, where most carries come from. TE2 needed nothing: he comes on in 12 personnel (the SLOT spot) and the heavy 22 sets (the Z spot) through the existing groupings. The box score now counts snaps for every man who lines up, and the grades show them.

### Post-game

**To the main menu.** The results screen's actions from a game are Main menu (focused) and New draft; Esc goes to the main menu too. No Locker Room, no Draft again with the same roster.

**Main menu.** New Draft is the big button (Bungee, the Classic/Film Room tag beside it, a hint for ← →). Then Daily Challenge, Quick Play, My Team (the old Locker Room entry, only when a roster is saved), Practice Field. Then, smaller: Last Game (only when a game exists; its panel shows the result, the score and the grade), History, How to Play, Settings. My Team shows the roster with its depth chart and the last game board; its stage button is New draft (there's no walk-out with the same roster).

**Results.** The score counts up and the result and grade stamp in the middle of the screen; at two seconds the header slides to the top and the box score loads under it, on the clock alone: a press in those two seconds does nothing.

**Box score.** The tabbed build is gone (the tabs and `useReportNav`). The earlier one-screen build (`git show 868cb49:src/ui/screens/ResultsScreen.tsx`: passing, rushing and receiving in one table set, the team line, the drives beside it) was the starting point; it grew into the broadcast layout the owner listed, on one screen with no scrolling at 16:9 from 1280×720 up:
- left: Passing (Comp–Att, Yards, Avg, TD, INT, Sacked, Long, Rating), Rushing (Carries, Yards, Avg, TD, Long, Broke tackles, Fumbles), Receiving (Catches, Targets, Yards, Avg, TD, Long, After catch, Drops), and Player grades (grade, role, name, snaps, the reason in one line);
- right: the Beasts' defense (Tackles, Sacks, Pressures, INT, Breakups, Big hits; the seven with the most impact, "and N others"), the drive chart (one row a round: the Beasts' result, then your start, plays, yards and result, with the team line in its header), and the big hits (the four hardest: round, who, on whom);
- one line above it all: the play of the game (still flagged for the M7 replay) and, in the Daily, the perfect-team tally.
Every column is a word a fan knows; no "SK", "BT", "YAC" or "PBU". Last Game, History and My Team's F key all open this same screen. Dropped from the old tabs: the per-lineman pass-block and run-block table, the coverage snapshot and the key matchups; the data is still in every record.

**Player grades.** The diagnosis and the new rule are below.

**Pre-game.** After the walk-out the game opens on a pre-game stage: both teams set at the opening spot (a display-only lineup that never steps, pushes no input context and uses none of the game's seeds), the score bug, a lower third with the matchup, the wind, "the Beasts get the ball first" and their eleven, and a "Kick off" prompt with the Enter / A key. The prompt arms after 0.9 s and a held key's repeats never count, so a button mashed through the walk-out can't run anything.

Also fixed on the way: a ball thrown away made a nameless receiving line (it's an attempt, not a target now); a touchdown drive's points left out the try (the chart read "Touchdown" without the +7).

## Grade diagnosis

The results screen graded with legacy's `gradeQB`, `gradeRush` and `gradeRec`, feeding them `yds × 10 / rounds` so a short game read like a ten-drive one. Three faults:
1. **The scaling inflated every rate.** It multiplied the yards but not the attempts, targets or carries. In a 4-round game (×2.5), 3 of 6 for 43 yards is 7.2 yards an attempt, but `gradeQB` saw 107.5 yards on 6 attempts, 17.9 an attempt, worth +23 on its own: 99, an A+. Unscaled the same line grades a C. A receiver's yards per catch inflated the same way. (`tests/grades.test.ts` reproduces the A+.)
2. **A QB's grade read only his passing line.** Scrambles went into a separate rushing row with its own grade, so 101 passing and 175 rushing graded as a 101-yard passer (below `gradeQB`'s 180-yard par: the B) next to an unrelated rushing grade.
3. **No touches got a flat C**, and a couple of touches could swing anywhere.

**Now** (`src/game/grades.ts`): one grade per player from everything he produced, against what his role makes in a game this long.
- *Value:* yards from scrimmage (a QB's passing net of sacks, plus his rushing; a back's rushing plus receiving) + 20 a touchdown − 45 a turnover (Pro Football Reference's adjusted-yards weights).
- *Expected:* the role's NFL per-game value (starting QB ≈ 238, lead back 94, change-of-pace back 48, WR1/2/3 81/58/39, TE1 48, TE2 12; 2018–23, approximate) ÷ 11 possessions × your possessions this game.
- *Score* = 80 + production + efficiency − 3 a drop, on legacy's letter scale (80 is a B−, 93 an A, 97 an A+). Production is 14 × log2 of the ratio (damped for small numbers), in [−18, +16], counted in proportion to n / (n + 4) of his touches so two balls his way can't make an A+ or an F. Efficiency is his rate against the league's (ANY/A 6.2, 4.3 a carry, 8.0 / 7.2 / 6.0 a target for receivers, tight ends, backs), shrunk when he had few chances, in ±10.
- *The reason*, one line: his line in words and the comparison, "24 of 35, 319 yards, 1 TD, 1 pick, 1 yard rushing: twice what a starting QB makes in 6 drives". The full trace is the row's tooltip.
- *No touches:* no grade, "No touches on 7 snaps".
The two playtest cases now: 3 of 6 for 43 as a QB in four drives is a C; as a WR1 it's a B. 101 passing, 175 rushing and no TDs in six drives is an A (it was a B that ignored the 175). In the fixture game: Montana A (24 of 35, 319, twice a starter's output), Rice A, Jones A, Clark B on one catch for 36, both backs D+ on 1.8 and 1.6 a carry, Russ Francis B+ on 1 of 3 for 34 (an A+ before the few-touches damping, which the first capture showed and prompted).

## Critique (from the stills)

*The draft.*
- `draft-pick-ol`: the O-line card is the best new thing. The five names with gold All-Pro and blue Pro Bowl stickers read like a media guide, and "ELITE" in lime says what a fan needs without a number. Larry Allen at LT is a data quirk (he played guard most of his career), not a UI one.
- `draft-pick-full`: the struck-through, greyed rows push the eye to the open positions exactly as asked; "RBs full" on the right says why.
- The wall's reels are readable: DAL and 1990S sit clean in their boxes with no halo. The unlocked rows above and below are dimmer than before, which reads as depth. Real-GPU bloom is the judge; these are software-rendered.
- `draft-complete-depth`: the strip turns into the depth chart with its "DEPTH CHART" kicker, WR1 Rison, WR2 Waddle, WR3 Jackson although Jackson was drafted before Waddle. The stall row stays in pick order, which is right for the room but means the chart and the stalls disagree by design; the rules card says why.
- `stall-*-stickers`, `stall-ol-stickers`: all the stickers are on (Allen: the tag and four traits; the Browns line: the tag, STRONG, three traits and 4× Pro Bowl), in Barlow Semi Condensed, which fits twice as much text as Bungee did. Weak: at the wide shot they're small; they read in the stall close-up only.
- `wall-beasts`: all eleven inside the frame, two columns, team and decade under each name. The page is less dense than before and reads better for it.
- `draft-rules-card`: five numbered lines on the left while the Beasts are on the wall, short enough to read while looking at the lineup; it goes on the first spin. Weak: the stage's old "Tonight: The Beasts" caption sits on the wall's page dots.
- `draft-reels`: the stage readout is plain near-white PIT and lilac 1990s in the condensed face, no glow; the wall's locked reels are crisp.

*Post-game.*
- `box-score-1080` / `box-score-720`: one screen, nothing clipped at 1280×720, no scrollbars. It looks like a broadcast stat page: the lime section heads, the numbers in a condensed face with tabular figures, the grades coloured by letter. The drive chart with the team line in its header is compact and tells the game's story. Weak: the right column ends two-thirds of the way down on a six-round game; the left is dense. A ten-round game with overtime fills the right column to about 90%. The reasons in the grade rows are long; at 1280 they fit but are small (0.92 rem).
- `main-menu`, `main-menu-last-game`: New Draft dominates as asked; the three tiers (hero, main, small) are clear. The Last Game panel shows the result, the score and the grade.
- `my-team`: My Team shows the roster, its depth chart and the last game board; the stage says New draft, with no way to replay the roster. Its F key opens the same box score component as the results. The still shows the Beasts page on the wall; My Team now opens on the row instead (fixed after this capture).
- `pregame`, `pregame-after-kickoff`: both teams set at the 25 from behind the offense, the score bug (0–0, Q1 15:00, round 1 of 6, the wind), the matchup card with the Beasts' eleven, and Kick off with its key. It reads as the moment before the game. Weak: it's the play-call camera, not an establishing shot of the Beasts, who are small at the far side of the line. After the press the Meanwhile cut takes over as before.
- Not captured: the results reveal's first two seconds. The shot harness skips reveals (they're rAF-timed and the software renderer's frames are too slow to catch a beat); it needs a look on real hardware.

## Sim changes this would need (not made)

- **RB2's share by design, not by rule of thumb.** The rotation is a game-layer rule (screens, passing downs, every third snap). A real change-of-pace back would come from the sim: fatigue per player (the play engine's fatigue is keyed by slot, so the starter's wear follows the RB slot, not the man), and the coordinator suggesting RB2's kind of plays when he's in (`sim/coordinator.ts` reads `team.RB` only).
- **The coordinator still reads RB2 as a fullback.** `strengths()` scores 21/22 personnel from `team.RB2`'s pass block and the lead-blocker trait. With the depth chart, RB2 is the lesser tailback; the draft has no fullback slot. A fullback (or "RB2 as FB only if he has the build") is a sim/personnel decision.
- **TE2's snaps depend on the plays called.** He plays in 12 and 22 personnel only; the Suggested tab doesn't favour those groupings for a strong TE2 (`suggestPlays`), so a player who never calls them never sees him. Weighting 12 personnel by the second tight end's quality is a coordinator change.
- **Snap counts for the Beasts** (who was in the nickel and dime) would need the sim to report substitutions; the box score counts only your side.
