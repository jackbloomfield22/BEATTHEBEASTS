# M6.5 brief (the owner's words, verbatim): the gameplay pass, after the M6 PR (#9)

After the M6 PR is open, the next pass is the gameplay, as M6.5 on its own branch. The passing game is the number one thing in the game and it doesn't feel like football yet. Seven problems, and I want each one diagnosed before it's fixed: find the cause in the sim, tell me what it was, then fix it.

1. Throws are unpredictable. The same throw to the same spot lands in different places for no reason I can see. The accuracy cone should be small for a good QB on a clean pocket and grow only for real reasons: pressure, throwing on the run, a long throw, a bad platform. Log every throw's intended point, actual point, and each error source, and make the error visibly attributable. A 95-accuracy QB with a clean pocket puts a 15-yard throw within a yard almost every time.

2. Receivers stop mid-route or switch routes. Find why: ball tracking kicking in too early, the scramble drill triggering when the QB just moved, a route re-plan on a sideline rule, or a state change on the openness read. A receiver runs the called route at full speed until the ball is in the air to him or the QB has actually left the pocket. Test it: on every play against every coverage, each receiver's path matches the route art until the throw.

3. Drops of wide-open passes. Open catches with a catchable ball should be nearly automatic (98% for good hands, 92% for poor). Drops come from contact, a ball behind the receiver, a bullet at short range, or bad hands, and the result card should say which.

4. No YAC threat. After the catch the receiver should already be at speed with a plan: a burst upfield, a move on the first defender if he's alone, the sideline when it's there. Defenders converge with real angles, not teleport-precise ones, so a receiver who beats his man gets the yards. Target average YAC on short routes 5 to 6, with a real tail of 20+ YAC plays.

5. Catch animations. Author the full set and wire them to the catch call: the over-the-shoulder catch on a deep ball, the high-point leap with hands above the helmet for GO UP, the body catch and go-down for SECURE, the on-the-run hands catch for RUN, a diving catch when the ball is low and away, a toe-tap on the sideline, a one-hander when the ball is high and outside. The catch call should change what I see, not just a probability.

6. Chemistry between QB and WR. Anticipation throws: the QB can throw to a spot before the receiver breaks, on timing routes (out, curl, comeback, slant, stick), and the receiver arrives as the ball does. Back-shoulder on a go when the corner is on top. The receiver adjusts to a slightly off throw with a body lean or a reach rather than stopping. Over a game, a receiver I keep going to gets a small timing bonus with my QB; the roster synergies feed this.

7. Receiver awareness. Receivers know where they are: they sit in the open window of a zone instead of running through it, come back to the ball on a scramble, work back toward the sticks on third down, toe the sideline instead of running out, and in the red zone they work the back line and the pylon. The AI should read as a player who knows the situation.

Harness and review: report before and after on completion, YAC distribution, drop rate by cause, throw error by source, and route fidelity. Then record ten plays from the broadcast camera with a different concept each (slant, out, curl, go, post, corner, crosser, screen, back-shoulder, scramble drill) and put them in docs/screenshots/m6.5. Watch each one and write down what still looks wrong before opening the PR. Football is art; judge it by whether the play looks like the play.

8. The run game has no home run threat. Diagnose why: blocks that never spring a lane, second-level defenders who always fill right, safeties who never lose leverage, or a carrier who can't get to top speed before contact. A back with 95 speed who gets through the line clean should be a threat to go the distance, and a great line against a bad front should open real lanes. Target 10+ runs around 11% with a real tail of 20+ and 40+, median carry around 3, and the boom-or-bust feel gone: more 4 to 7 yard runs, fewer stuffs and fewer breakaways at once.

9. Carrier controls, same idea as the catch call. Show three options at a time out of the six moves (juke, stiff arm, spin, truck, dive, protect), chosen by the situation and mapped to 1, 2, 3 in that moment: a defender coming from the side offers juke, stiff arm, spin; one square in front offers truck, spin, juke; in the open field offers dive, protect and the best move for the nearest angle. Large, one word each, rotating as the picture changes, with the one I press lighting up. Whatever's in slot 1 is always the move most likely to work.

10. Lateral movement with the arrows is bad. Right now it's hard to get sideways or around a defender. Fix the steering: a direction change at speed becomes a real cut (plant, turn, accelerate out) at a cost set by Agility, not a slow drift, and a quick opposite-direction tap is a hard cut. Turning rate scales with Agility and speed. The controlled-speed rule near defenders must not make the carrier sluggish; it should make cuts sharper, not slower. Measure: time from an arrow press to a 90-degree change of direction at full speed, target under 0.4 s for a 90-agility back.

11. Ball-carrier animation and awareness. The run cycles from M4.5 are fine for a receiver on a route; a ball carrier moves differently and right now he doesn't. Author a carrier set: the ball tucked with the off arm working, a lower center of gravity, shorter choppier steps in traffic and a long stride in space, the head up and scanning, a lean into the hole, a plant-and-cut with a real weight shift, a shoulder dip before contact, the lower-the-pad truck, a hurdle over a downed player, a dive that reaches the ball out at the pylon or the sticks. Blends between all of them driven by the sim's state, so a cut looks like a cut and a burst looks like a burst.

And the carrier knows where he is: he sees the sideline and turns upfield or steps out on his own terms, sees the first-down marker and reaches for it, sees the pylon and extends, sees a lane and hits it, sees a wall and cuts back. The AI carrier and the player-controlled carrier share this read layer; when I'm steering, it shapes his footwork and his lean under my inputs rather than overriding them.
